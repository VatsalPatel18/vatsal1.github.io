Project 1

MedDiscover: The RAG Framework

Status

Major Revision (CSBJ)

Domain-Specificity
wins against General Models.

We built a RAG pipeline tailored for Metabolomics (ICD-10 E70-E88). Hypothesized that general embeddings (Ada-002) fail at complex metabolite interactions.

Key Discovery: "Minimal Words"

NotebookLM performed poorly until prompted for "Minimal Words", spiking scores. We enforced a 25-token limit in MedDiscover, proving that constraints improve precision in biomedical QA.

Project 3

Abliteration & Safety

Frontier

New Risk Surface

"Refusal" is a Physical
Direction in the Model.

Post-2024 research (Arditi et al.) revealed that a model's refusal mechanism (e.g., "I cannot answer that") is mediated by a single 1D subspace in the residual stream.

Method 1: Ablation

Removing the Brake Material

By surgically removing this "refusal direction", we can "abliterate" the safety mechanism without retraining the model.

Method 2: Addition

Injecting Refusal

Conversely, adding this direction can force a model to refuse even harmless prompts (e.g., "Hello").

Refusal
Direction
Project 3

Proposal: The "Latent Guard"

Can we separate "Harmfulness"
from "Refusal"?

Abliteration shows that refusal is fragile. But does the model still know the content is harmful, even if it answers?

Research Hypothesis

The model retains a "Harmfulness" concept that is orthogonal to the "Refusal" direction. Steering along 'harmfulness' changes judgment, while steering 'refusal' just changes compliance.

Proposed Output

A research review paper tracking governments' and safety orgs' ability to monitor "abliterated" models. If the guardrails are gone, can we still detect the harmful intent latently?
