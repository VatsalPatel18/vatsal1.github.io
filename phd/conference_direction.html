Concept 1: Physician Intent Preservation

Primary-work extension of physician-intent modeling in clinical dialogue

Closest To Alexander's Direction
Working Title

Can small local LLMs preserve physician intent in doctor-patient dialogue?

What can be done

  • The dataset crafted in "Where does it hurt?" can be used as the empirical base and evaluated with medically augmented LLMs such as MedGemma, MedPhi, and MedLlama.
  • These models can be quantized for edge-device deployment and analyzed systematically across INT4, INT8, and FP16 settings.
  • Multiple LLMs can be organized within a central agentic structure for next-intent classification and comparative evaluation.

How does it align?

  • The concept remains closely aligned with Alexander Loeser's existing dataset and research direction.
  • The study would constitute a small but independent primary research paper rather than a review article.
  • The results can provide preliminary evidence for clinically useful low-resource modeling before the broader PhD agenda is expanded.

Why it is feasible

The published work already defines the Aci-bench dataset and reports 5000+ annotated doctor-patient turns with a SOAP-based physician-intent taxonomy, making the study primarily an evaluation task rather than a new data-collection effort.

MedGemma MedLlama MedPhi Encoder baseline

A meaningful novelty angle is the comparison between generic baselines and medically aligned models under compression, rather than model comparison alone.

Concept 2: High-Stakes Benchmark Slice

Emergency and low-resource clinical evaluation for small LLMs

Dataset Creation & Benchmarking Anchor
Working Title

Do small clinical LLMs fail safely in emergency and low-resource scenarios?

What can be done

  • Following the logic of "Where does it hurt?", a focused sub-dataset can be created for adverse emergency scenarios; if the resulting sample is insufficient, HealthBench and AMEGA can be used as supporting public resources.
  • A set of 3-5 small open models can then be evaluated on this benchmark slice.
  • The evaluation can report not only score, but also unsafe omission rate, overconfidence, escalation / referral behavior, and latency.

How does it align?

  • Dataset creation and benchmarking studies remain a well-recognized pattern in conference publications.
  • It introduces the trustworthiness layer explicitly requested in the doctoral framing, including uncertainty, failure behavior, and safety.
  • The sub-dataset strategy mirrors an established and feasible paper pattern: define a focused slice, benchmark it carefully, and use the results to motivate deeper follow-up work.
Feasibility base

HealthBench is built around 5,000 realistic health conversations with physician-written rubrics and includes emergency referrals and uncertainty handling. AMEGA already benchmarks guideline adherence across multiple medical specialties. This makes the study primarily a targeted benchmark-construction and evaluation exercise, rather than a new data-collection project.

Evaluation outputs
  • Overall score
  • Unsafe omission rate
  • Overconfidence
  • Poor escalation / referral behavior
  • Latency
Best venue
AIME short paper, with BHI as the operational backup route if required.
What this becomes later
This direction can establish thesis objective 2 as the benchmark core. The same slice can later support objective 1 through the evaluation of quantization effects on reliability and clinical safety.
